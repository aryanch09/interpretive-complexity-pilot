Variance decomposition of window-level disagreement
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  How much of the variance in window-level disagreement sits BETWEEN firms,
  BETWEEN filings within a firm, and WITHIN filings.  Reported three ways,
  because no single one of them is both assumption-free and unbiased:

   (i) descriptive nested sums of squares on the raw measure - needs no
       model and no convergence, but the between-firm share also contains
       whatever the fixed effects explain (tier is a between-firm variable),
       and under unbalanced group sizes the higher-level shares are biased
       UPWARD because each group mean carries residual noise;
  (ii) the same decomposition on the residuals from the fixed-effect fit,
       which removes (i)'s first problem but not its second;
 (iii) the REML variance components from specification (a) - these are the
       actual estimates, and the ones to quote, when (a) converges.

  SUBSTANTIVE READ (basis: descriptive SS shares on the raw measure)
       78.3% of the variance in window-level disagreement sits
      WITHIN filings.  Filing-level means are therefore mostly
      averaging noise, and the paper should say so explicitly: a
      filing's headline number is a weak summary of a highly
      variable within-document quantity, and the firm-level means
      the confirmatory test uses inherit that noise.

Limitations of W5 as specified
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * (1 | firm/filing) is approximated, not fitted literally: MixedLM has
    one grouping factor and expresses the filing level as a variance
    component inside the firm group.
  * MixedLM CIs are Wald/normal; no Satterthwaite or Kenward-Roger
    correction is available, so with ~30 firms they are optimistic.
  * 30 clusters is few for cluster-robust inference in (c).
  * W5 is one row of the section 5 battery. The section 5 overall gate
    requires W3 and W5 to pass and W2 not to be contradicted; W5 alone
    neither establishes nor refutes H1.
  * The confirmatory unit of analysis remains the FIRM (n=30, test C1).
    W5 is a window-level diagnostic, not the confirmatory test.
